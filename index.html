The Open-Air Gym: How Australians Exercise Outdoors

Walking, running, bushwalking and cycling in parks, streets, trails and beaches.

Parks, footpaths, trails and beaches are Australia’s most popular places to exercise. In 2025, 62 in every 100 adults used them.

Using data from four public organisations, this page follows five questions about outdoor fitness: who, where, what, when and why.

Who — Who Exercises Outdoors?

AusPlay, the national survey of sport and physical activity, asks adults where they were active in the past year. Parks, streets, trails and beaches count as public spaces.

Outdoor exercise rises through middle age. It peaks at 72% for ages 55–64, then falls to 56% after 65.

Every state and territory exercises mostly outdoors, but the share differs. Chart 2 compares the share of adults active in public spaces in each state.

Activities — What Do They Do?

This page follows four outdoor activities: walking, bushwalking, running and cycling. Organised sports and swimming appear in grey for context.

Walking for exercise has been first every year since 2016, and bushwalking is now second. Who does each activity depends on sex and age.

Seasons — When and How Often?

Heat and rain decide when it is pleasant to exercise outside. Chart 8 counts the days at 30°C or hotter in each capital city, month by month.

Chart 9 shows how often adults are active. In chart 10, choose a city to find its mildest months.

Motives — Why Go Out, and What Stops People?

Health and fitness is the most common reason to be active (67% of active adults). Still, 51% also exercise to be outdoors in nature.

For people who are not active, the top barriers are poor health or injury (27%) and feeling too old (23%). The weather stops only 5%.

Conclusion

Public spaces are where Australians exercise most: 62 in 100 adults used one in 2025. Walking leads every year, bushwalking is rising, and outdoor exercise peaks at 55 to 64.

Yet the National Health Survey 2022 found that just 24% of people aged 15 and over met the national guidelines. Free stations (chart 3) and mild months (chart 10) make starting easier.

Notes on the data

Activity names are in plain English. Organised sport and swimming appear only in grey, for context. Shares are survey estimates and are rounded. A “point” is the difference between two percentages: 61% to 73% is 12 points.

AusPlay moved from phone interviews to an online survey in July 2023, so results from 2016–2022 and from 2024 onwards are not directly comparable. Chart 5 keeps the two periods separate.

OpenStreetMap is mapped by volunteers, so charts 3 and 4 show mapped stations only.
